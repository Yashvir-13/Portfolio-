# PORTFOLIO — DESIGN & CREATIVE DIRECTION

## Core idea

This is a personal archive, not a conventional developer portfolio.

The central idea is:

> I make things to understand things.

The site should feel like a personal archive someone discovered at 2 AM — intimate, cinematic, analogue, quiet, slightly uncanny, dreamy, and deeply personal.

A useful mental model:

> an old archive from a future that never happened

The website itself should feel like one of the things its owner made.

---

# EXPERIENCE

The visitor should feel that they are moving through someone's collected thoughts and artifacts rather than browsing a professional résumé.

The site contains different forms of making:
- software
- films
- photography
- poetry
- essays/fragments
- philosophical notes
- experiments
- unfinished ideas

These should not feel like unrelated portfolio categories. They are different ways of investigating the same recurring questions:

- time
- memory
- perception
- consciousness
- technology
- loneliness
- dreams
- reality
- observation
- creation

The site should communicate curiosity rather than achievement.

---

# AESTHETIC

Desired feeling:
- cinematic
- melancholic
- intimate
- analogue
- dreamy
- mysterious
- slightly surreal
- human
- restrained
- contemplative
- spatial

References in feeling rather than imitation:
- David Lynch's dream logic / uncanny normality
- Radiohead's emotional and visual atmosphere
- cinema
- old archives
- darkrooms
- notebooks
- film negatives
- empty physical spaces
- late-night observation

Do NOT turn this into:
- horror
- cyberpunk
- futuristic UI
- glitch art
- excessive VHS effects
- CRT effects
- particle effects
- neon
- "AI aesthetic"
- generic minimalist developer portfolio
- SaaS landing page
- dashboard
- résumé website

Surrealism should come from **composition, pacing, imagery, scale, and context**, not from effects.

---

# VISUAL LANGUAGE

## Color

Current palette:

- background: `#0e0d0b`
- primary text: `#e0ddd7`
- accent: `#732626`

The overall visual language should remain warm rather than pure black/cold white.

Use subtly different dark surfaces between sections when useful rather than making everything the exact same black.

The muted dark red should remain restrained.

---

## Typography

Use a combination of:
- expressive serif typography
- small monospace metadata

The serif carries the human/literary/cinematic voice.

The monospace carries:
- dates
- labels
- coordinates
- technical metadata
- section markers
- small archive information

Typography should have room to breathe.

Large type can become a visual object.

Tiny metadata can create the feeling of an archival document.

---

# COMPOSITION

The most important design principle is **composition over effects**.

Use:
- asymmetry
- unusual alignment
- large areas of negative space
- oversized type
- partial crops
- images extending beyond expected boundaries
- objects placed off-center
- visual interruptions
- changes in scale
- long quiet sections
- occasional dense information

Avoid making every section into the same centered container.

Break symmetry deliberately.

The site should sometimes feel almost empty.

---

# HOMEPAGE

The homepage should feel edited like a short film.

Conceptual sequence:

### Shot 01 — YASHVIR

Opening should be extremely sparse.

Large:

> YASHVIR

Small archival information around it.

The visitor should not immediately be presented with a conventional bio.

---

### Shot 02 — Image

Use the strongest available photograph.

Not a headshot.

The image should establish the world of the archive.

Possible imagery:
- night sky
- empty room
- corridor
- window
- reflection
- road
- projector
- architectural space
- ordinary object made strange by framing

Large image + lots of surrounding darkness.

---

### Shot 03 — Statement

Large statement:

> I make things to understand things.

Give this room to breathe.

This should feel like a declaration rather than a tagline.

---

### Shot 04 — Latest / selected work

Feature an important project visually.

Asteria is a particularly strong candidate.

Do not present it as:
- problem
- solution
- features
- tech stack

Instead, present it as an artifact.

Asteria should feel like something found inside the archive.

Use:
- screenshot
- fragments of interface
- typography
- unusual crop
- surrounding metadata

The project should be experienced before it is explained.

---

### Shot 05 — Interruption

A short line can appear alone in a large empty space.

Example direction:

> Some things are made because they cannot be explained.

This section should function like a pause.

---

### Shot 06 — Film

Feature a film still from:

> Untitled (Isolation)

The section should feel like cinema rather than a video-platform embed.

Use:
- large still
- title
- year
- duration
- very small metadata
- director's note

The image should dominate.

---

### Shot 07 — Writing

Feature a piece such as:

> Again

Treat the writing as a manuscript.

Do not make it look like a blog card.

Use:
- large title
- sparse excerpt
- generous whitespace
- perhaps a fragment of text
- small date/type metadata

---

### Shot 08 — Photography

Show several photographs.

Not a conventional 3-column gallery.

Use:
- different image sizes
- partial crops
- large single photographs
- contact-sheet-like arrangements
- negative space

The photography should interrupt the typography and architecture of the page.

---

### Shot 09 — Notes

A quiet list of observations.

Examples:
- On Time
- On Color
- On Cinema

Keep this restrained.

---

### Shot 10 — Not Yet

Unfinished things should appear as part of the archive.

Examples:
- Untitled Film Idea
- 37 Lines
- Computer Vision Experiment

This section should feel like a desk containing unfinished work.

Not a "coming soon" marketing section.

---

### Footer

Quiet, minimal, archival.

The site should end rather than advertise.

# SECTION "ROOMS"

Each major section can have its own physical/mental environment while remaining part of the same world.

## Home
Dark room / archive.

## Work
Studio / laboratory / artifact table.

Should feel like things are being examined.

## Films
Cinema.

Large frames, darkness, sparse metadata.

## Photography
Darkroom / contact sheets.

Images dominate.

## Writing
Paper / manuscript.

Typography dominates.

## Notes
Notebook.

Quiet observations and fragments.

## Not Yet
Desk with unfinished work.

Loose, incomplete, unresolved.

## About
Almost empty room.

The simplest and most human section.

These should be subtle shifts, not completely different websites.

---

# IMAGERY

Real imagery is essential to the identity.

Use actual:
- photography
- film stills
- Asteria screenshots
- project artifacts
- physical spaces
- objects
- textures

Images should be structural elements, not decorative thumbnails.

Do not make everything a card with:
`image → title → description → button`.

Instead, allow images to occupy the page as compositions.

---

# PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION

Photography should feel like a visual diary.

Potential subjects:
- night sky
- empty spaces
- rooms
- corridors
- roads
- reflections
- projector light
- dust
- windows
- fluorescent lighting
- architectural details
- ordinary places at unusual times

The photographs do not need to explain themselves.

Captions can be poetic and minimal.

Known examples:
- "The sky always looks heavier at night."
- "Dust caught in projector beam."
- "A Room"

The photography page should feel closer to a darkroom/contact sheet than an online shop or stock-photo gallery.

---

# FILM DIRECTION

Film should be treated as cinema.

For each film:
- large still / visual opening
- title
- year
- duration
- sparse metadata
- description
- director's note
- optional video

Do not lead with a YouTube embed.

The still should establish the film first.

---

# WRITING DIRECTION

Writing should feel like a manuscript or discovered document.

Use:
- serif typography
- generous margins
- large whitespace
- minimal navigation
- small archival metadata
- text as the primary visual element

Avoid:
- blog cards
- social-media styling
- publication dashboards
- excessive UI

Writing can include:
- poetry
- essays
- fragments

---

# NOTES DIRECTION

Notes are observations rather than conventional articles.

Examples:
- On Time
- On Color
- On Cinema

The presentation should resemble a personal notebook/archive.

Small dates and metadata can be used.

The writing itself should remain central.

---

# NOT YET DIRECTION

"Not Yet" is one of the site's important conceptual sections.

It contains:
- unfinished projects
- abandoned ideas
- fragments
- experiments
- things that may never become complete

It should not apologize for being unfinished.

The design can feel:
- loose
- incomplete
- slightly scattered
- desk-like
- provisional

But still intentional.

---

# WORK DIRECTION

Work should not become a standard engineering portfolio.

The technical work is part of the same creative archive.

Selected projects should be given more visual and conceptual space.

A project page can contain:
- title
- statement
- visual artifact
- overview
- process
- technical details
- lessons
- links

But the technical information should come after the artifact/idea has established itself.

Asteria should particularly feel like an object discovered in the archive.

---

# MOTIF

A subtle recurring timestamp can appear throughout the site:

> 02:17

It should be:
- unexplained
- rare
- subtle
- never turned into a gimmick

It can appear like an archival timestamp, frame marker, or incidental piece of information.

Do not overuse it.

---

# MOTION

Motion should feel like film editing, not interface animation.

Prefer:
- slow transitions
- deliberate reveals
- subtle opacity changes
- gentle movement
- long pauses
- image transitions

Avoid:
- bouncing elements
- excessive parallax
- scroll-jacking
- constant animations
- flashy transitions
- glitch transitions

The page should still work beautifully with motion reduced or disabled.

---

# NAVIGATION

Desktop navigation can remain:
- fixed
- small
- monospace
- uppercase
- understated
- visually integrated with the page

Current concept uses subtle `mix-blend-mode: difference`.

Navigation should not dominate.

On mobile, do NOT force eight navigation links into a cramped wrapped row.

Prefer a compact index/menu.

---

# GRAIN / TEXTURE

A very subtle film-grain/noise layer is appropriate.

It should be barely perceptible.

The existing idea of roughly 0.04 opacity is appropriate.

Use texture as atmosphere, not as an effect that announces itself.

Do not add:
- VHS distortion
- scanlines
- CRT curvature
- RGB splitting
- glitching
- digital noise overload

---

# RESPONSIVE DESIGN

The atmosphere must survive mobile.

Mobile should prioritize:
- readable typography
- intentional image crops
- vertical pacing
- generous whitespace
- compact navigation
- no horizontal overflow
- no tiny unreadable metadata

Asymmetry can remain, but it should become deliberate rather than broken.

---

# MEDIA / PERFORMANCE

The site is a personal archive, so infrastructure should remain simple.

Current environment:
- GitHub
- Vercel
- Neon
- repository-hosted media

Cloudflare R2 is intentionally NOT part of the current design/environment.

Images should:
- be compressed
- be appropriately resized
- avoid unnecessarily huge originals
- use modern formats where practical
- lazy-load when below the fold
- use responsive image sizing where appropriate

The first viewport should load only what is necessary.

Performance should come from sensible assets and layout, not from stripping away the site's visual character.

---

# CONTENT PHILOSOPHY

Do not invent content to fill space.

The archive should be honest.

The user makes:
- software
- films
- photographs
- poetry
- notes
- experiments
- unfinished work

Not everything needs to be presented as a major achievement.

Small things can remain small.

Unfinished things can remain unfinished.

The site is a record of making, not a résumé of accomplishments.

---

# PERSONAL IDENTITY

The website should make the different disciplines feel connected.

Software, cinema, photography, poetry and philosophy are not separate brands.

They are different instruments for looking at the world.

The recurring emotional territory is:
- observing
- remembering
- questioning
- making
- trying to understand

The site should feel like the person behind the work is present even when there is very little biographical text.

---

# ENVIRONMENT / TECHNICAL DIRECTION

Keep the implementation simple and maintainable.

Current intended environment:

GitHub
→ source code and media

Vercel
→ hosting / deployment

Neon
→ structured content and CMS data

External platforms
→ Medium / Substack / YouTube where relevant

No unnecessary infrastructure.

The design should remain the priority.

---

# FINAL DESIGN TEST

Before approving a design change, ask:

1. Does this feel like an archive rather than a portfolio template?
2. Does it feel cinematic?
3. Is there enough negative space?
4. Is the composition asymmetrical where appropriate?
5. Does imagery feel structural rather than decorative?
6. Does it feel personal?
7. Does it avoid generic SaaS/developer aesthetics?
8. Is the surrealism coming from composition rather than effects?
9. Does the page feel like a physical place?
10. Would this still feel interesting if all animations were removed?

The goal is not to make the site look "cool."

The goal is to make the site feel **like a place that could only belong to its creator**.
